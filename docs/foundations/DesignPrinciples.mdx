# Design principles

These principles guide how Serupa's components, tokens and docs are made, and how the screens built with them should behave. Use them to decide cases a guide doesn't cover, and to settle design reviews.

They are for weighing choices, not a checklist to tick. Start every screen from what it is for and who it is for, then let the principles help you choose between good options. When they conflict, accessibility comes first.

## Accessible

Everyone can use it, whatever their device, input or ability.

- Every component meets WCAG 2.2 AA in every theme, and tests check it.
- Color is never the only signal. A status has a word; a chart series has a shape.
- Targets are at least 24px, and 44px on touch screens.
- Plan for it from the first sketch. Accessibility added at the end costs more and is never as good.

## Adaptable

Work the way each person works, wherever they are.

- Every action works with a mouse, touch, a keyboard and voice: a visible label that matches the accessible name, nothing that only appears on hover, and no gesture without a button that does the same.
- Keep things in their place as the screen changes. The same control sits in the same spot at every width, so people don't have to find it again.
- Bring people back to where they were. A reload, a narrow window or a return visit keeps the tab, the filter and the scroll they left. See [Keeping state](?path=/docs/patterns-page-shell--docs).
- Give each width its own care: the phone layout is designed, not the desktop one squeezed.

  doText="Keep row actions in a visible button."
  doExample={<Source language="tsx" dark={false} code={'<IconButton icon="more_horiz" label="More actions for Invoice 1042" />\n// reachable by touch, keyboard and voice ("click More actions")'} />}
  dontText="Show actions only on hover."
  dontExample={<Source language="css" dark={false} code={'.row .actions { opacity: 0; }\n.row:hover .actions { opacity: 1; }\n/* nothing to tap on a phone, nothing to name by voice */'} />}

## Native

Build on what the browser already does well.

- Use native elements first: `<select>`, `<dialog>`, `<input>`. They bring the phone's picker, autofill and screen-reader support with them.
- Add newer CSS and HTML as progressive enhancement, with today's behavior as the fallback.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Style the native control."

/>

## Clear

Say what things do, in the words people use.

- Label actions with what they do: "Delete project", not "OK".
- Follow conventions people already know: × closes, Escape cancels, Mod+Z undoes, the main action comes last. A familiar pattern beats a clever one.
- Show what can be used and what just changed: a control looks pressable, a disabled one says why, and a change on screen is announced.
- Use one word for one thing across the system. See [Content and voice](?path=/docs/foundations-content-and-voice--docs).

<DoDontExample
  doText="Name buttons after their action."
  doExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 8 }}><Button variant="outline">Keep project</Button><Button destructive>Delete project</Button></div>}
  dontText="Use Yes and No."
  dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 8 }}><Button variant="outline">No</Button><Button destructive>Yes</Button></div>}
/>

## Focused

Show what matters and leave out the rest.

- Know what the screen is for, and make that one job excellent before adding a second.
- One primary action per view.
- Show fewer options up front, and the rest on request.
- Simple is not the same as bare. Keep what people need close at hand, and move the rest one step away, rather than hiding everything.
- Add decoration only when it carries meaning. Keep motion short, and turn it off for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

  doText="Make one action primary."
  doExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 8 }}><Button variant="outline">Cancel</Button><Button variant="secondary">Preview</Button><Button>Publish</Button></div>}
  dontText="Make every action primary."
  dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 8 }}><Button>Cancel</Button><Button>Preview</Button><Button>Publish</Button></div>}

## In control

People choose how to reach their goal, and can always change their mind.

- Get out of the way: open on the task or the content, not on a welcome screen, a tour or a banner.
- Never trap people in a flow. A tour, an onboarding checklist or a multi-step form can be left, skipped or finished later, and keeps what was done.
- Make mistakes cheap. Act at once and offer Undo rather than asking "Are you sure?"; confirm only what can't be undone. See [Destructive actions](?path=/docs/guides-destructive-actions--docs).
- Going back costs nothing: Back, Cancel and Escape return to where people were with their work intact.

## Respectful

Act in the interest of the people using it, and earn their trust.

- Ask only for what the task needs. Every field is a cost to the person and a risk to keep.
- Say why before you ask: next to a field that isn't obvious, and before the browser's prompt for the camera, location or notifications. See [Permission request](?path=/docs/patterns-permission-request--docs).
- Be plain about what happens to people's data, and make the private choice the default.
- Leaving is as easy as joining: cancelling, unsubscribing and deleting an account take no more steps than signing up.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Say why you ask, and make it optional when it is."
  doExample={<div style={{ inlineSize: 260 }}><Field label="Phone" optional hint="For signing in if you lose your password."><Input type="tel" /></Field></div>}
  dontText="Require details the task doesn't need."
  dontExample={<div style={{ inlineSize: 260 }}><Field label="Phone" required><Input type="tel" /></Field></div>}
/>

## Complete

Design every state, not just the ideal one.

- Each component covers rest, hover, focus, pressed, invalid, read-only, disabled, loading and empty, at every size and in every theme.
- Errors say how to fix the problem.
- Sizes line up across components: a 32px input sits next to a 32px button.
- Care shows in the details people don't name: the corner that nests, the number that doesn't jump, the word that fits. Release is not the end; keep raising the bar.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Say how to fix the error."
  doExample={<div style={{ inlineSize: 260 }}><Field label="Email" error="Enter an email with an @, like ana@example.com."><Input type="email" defaultValue="ana.example.com" /></Field></div>}
  dontText="Only say that something is wrong."
  dontExample={<div style={{ inlineSize: 260 }}><Field label="Email" error="Invalid input."><Input type="email" defaultValue="ana.example.com" /></Field></div>}
/>

## Honest

Show what is actually happening.

- Respond to input right away.
- Show progress that reflects real work, and announce the result to screen readers.
- Don't fake it: no progress on a timer, no "Saved" before the save finishes.

  doText="Report real progress."

/>

## Lightweight

Ship only what the page needs.

- No runtime dependencies beyond React.
- Each component has a size budget, checked in CI.
- Icons and fonts load only the parts a page uses.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Import the components you use."
  doExample={<Source language="tsx" dark={false} code={"import { Button, Field, Input } from '@diki-stwn/serupa-ds';\n// fades and slides come with the components, from the motion tokens"} />}
  dontText="Add a library for one effect."
  dontExample={<Source language="tsx" dark={false} code={"import { motion } from 'some-animation-library';\n\n<motion.div animate={{ opacity: 1 }} /> // tens of KB for one fade"} />}
/>

## Consistent

Solve the same problem the same way.

- Components use tokens, never raw values, and a test checks it.
- Similar components share sizes, states and keys.
- Changes follow semantic versioning. A renamed prop keeps its old name, with a warning, until the next major.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Use tokens."
  doExample={<Source language="css" dark={false} code={'.card {\n  padding: var(--spacing-4);\n  color: var(--text-muted);\n  border-radius: var(--radius-surface);\n}'} />}
  dontText="Hard-code values."
  dontExample={<Source language="css" dark={false} code={'.card {\n  padding: 15px;\n  color: #6b7280;\n  border-radius: 7px;\n}'} />}
/>

## Human

Leave people feeling capable, not processed.

- Decide how a screen should feel: calm for money and health, quick for daily work, warm for a first visit. Let that choose the words, the motion and the pace.
- Small moments carry the character: an empty list with a next step, an error that helps, a confirmation that says what happens next.
- Delight comes from the task going well, not from decoration. Never let a flourish slow people down or get in the way.

  doText="Give an empty screen a way forward."
  doExample={<EmptyState size="sm" icon="folder_open" title="No projects yet" description="Projects keep a team's files and tasks together." actions={<Button size="sm">Create a project</Button>} />}
  dontText="Celebrate routine actions."
  dontExample={<Source language="tsx" dark={false} code={"onSave={() => {\n  launchConfetti(); // on every save\n  toast('Woohoo! You did it!');\n}}"} />}

## Influences

These principles draw on Dieter Rams' ten principles for good design, Jakob Nielsen's usability heuristics, the Gestalt principles, the Laws of UX, Microsoft's inclusive design, Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, and the design principles of Shopify Polaris, IBM Carbon, Atlassian, Google Material and Vercel Geist.
