# Design principles

Eight principles behind every component, token and guide in Serupa. Use them to choose between two good answers, in a review, and when a guide doesn't cover your case.

**Accessibility wins.** When two principles pull apart, the answer that works for more people is the one we take: a visible label over a cleaner look, a 24px target over a tighter row, words over a color that says it alone.

## 1. Everyone, from the start

Design for the edges and the middle is covered. People use Serupa with a keyboard, a screen reader, a phone in one hand, 200% zoom, a slow connection or a color they can't tell apart.

**What it means here:** every component meets WCAG 2.2 AA in all six themes, checked by tests, not by eye. Meaning never rests on color alone: a status has a word, a chart series has a shape. Targets are at least 24px, and 44px on touch.

## 2. Built on the platform

The browser already knows how to be a select, a dialog or a form. We style it and fill the gaps, and keep what it gives for free.

**What it means here:** native elements first (`<select>`, `<dialog>`, `<input type="date">` where it fits), so the phone's picker, autofill, find in page and assistive technology work without extra code. New CSS and HTML features come in as progressive enhancement, with today's behavior as the fallback.

## 3. Clear before clever

People should know what something does before they use it, in the words they would use themselves. Familiar beats novel: people spend most of their time in other products, and expect ours to work the same way.

**What it means here:** labels name what happens ("Delete project", not "OK"). Patterns follow what people already know: a × closes, Escape cancels, the main action comes last. One word means one thing across the whole system (see [Content and voice](?path=/docs/foundations-content-and-voice--docs)).

<DoDontExample
  doText="Name each button for what it does."
  doExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 8 }}><Button variant="outline">Keep project</Button><Button destructive>Delete project</Button></div>}

  dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 8 }}><Button variant="outline">No</Button><Button destructive>Yes</Button></div>}
/>

## 4. Quiet until it matters

Every element on a screen competes for attention. The fewer there are, the faster people find the one they need. Design stays in the background until there's something to say.

**What it means here:** one primary action per view. Fewer choices shown at once, more on request (a Menu, a "Show more"). Decoration only when it carries meaning: no shadow, gradient or animation for its own sake. Motion is short, has a reason, and stops for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 5. Every state, every case

A component is finished when its worst moment is designed, not its best one: the empty list, the long name, the slow network, the error, the right-to-left page.

**What it means here:** each component designs and tests rest, hover, focus, active, invalid, read-only, disabled, loading and empty, at every size, in every theme, in four languages and in forced colors. Errors say how to put things right. Sizes line up across components, so a 32px input sits beside a 32px button.

## 6. Honest and responsive

Show what is really happening, as soon as it happens. An answer within about 400ms keeps people in the flow; past that, they need to see that work is under way and how far it has got.

**What it means here:** a press answers at once. Waiting shows a skeleton or a progress that reflects real work, and finishing is announced to screen readers too. Nothing pretends: no fake progress, no "Saved" before the save, no control that looks pressable and isn't.

<DoDontExample

  doExample={<div style={{ inlineSize: 260 }}><Progress value={40} label="Uploading 4 of 10 files" /></div>}

## 7. Light by default

Every kilobyte is downloaded, parsed and run on someone's phone, often on a slow network and an old battery. A design system multiplies that cost across every page that uses it.

**What it means here:** no runtime dependencies beyond React. Each component is held to a size budget in CI, and imports only what it uses. Icons and fonts load only the parts a page needs. Animation uses the compositor and stops for reduced motion.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Import the components you use, and let their own CSS do the animating."
  doExample={<Source language="tsx" dark={false} code={"import { Button, Field, Input } from 'serupa-ds';\n// fades and slides come with the components, from the motion tokens"} />}
  dontText="Pull in a library for one effect: every visitor downloads all of it."
  dontExample={<Source language="tsx" dark={false} code={"import { motion } from 'some-animation-library';\n\n<motion.div animate={{ opacity: 1 }} /> // tens of KB for one fade"} />}
/>

## 8. Consistent by construction, careful with change

Consistency comes from shared parts, not from discipline: when every color, space and size is a token, a whole product moves together. And because products build on Serupa, every change to it is a change to them.

**What it means here:** components use tokens, never raw values (a test checks it). Similar things look and behave alike, from the size scale to the keys. Changes follow semantic versioning: a rename keeps the old name as a deprecated alias with a warning until the next major, and every change comes with a changelog entry.

<DoDontExample
  doText="Use tokens, so themes, dark mode and brands follow."
  doExample={<Source language="css" dark={false} code={'.card {\n  padding: var(--spacing-4);\n  color: var(--text-muted);\n  border-radius: var(--radius-surface);\n}'} />}
  dontText="Hard-code values: they drift from the system and stay the same color in dark mode."
  dontExample={<Source language="css" dark={false} code={'.card {\n  padding: 15px;\n  color: #6b7280;\n  border-radius: 7px;\n}'} />}
/>

## Influences

These are our own words, drawn from work we admire: Dieter Rams' ten principles for good design; Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics; the Gestalt principles of grouping; the Laws of UX (Fitts's, Hick's and Jakob's laws, the Doherty threshold); Microsoft's inclusive design; and the principles of Shopify Polaris, IBM Carbon, Atlassian, Google Material and Vercel Geist.
