# Design principles

Five principles guide how Serupa's components, tokens and docs are made, and how the screens built with them should behave. Use them to decide cases a guide doesn't cover, and to settle design reviews.

They are for weighing choices, not a checklist to tick. Each one gives the idea, then what it means in practice. When they conflict, accessibility comes first.

## Give everything a reason to exist

Know what people came to do, and let everything on the screen serve that.

**In practice**

- Start from who the screen is for and what they came to do. One primary action per view.
- Open on the task or the content, not on a welcome screen, a tour or a banner.
- Use plain words, and name each action for what it does: "Delete project", not "OK". See [Content and voice](?path=/docs/foundations-content-and-voice--docs).

## Earn trust in every interaction

People give a product their time, their effort and their data, so treat all three with honesty and care.

**In practice**

- Progress reflects real work, and "Saved" appears only after the save finishes.
- Act at once and offer Undo rather than asking "Are you sure?"; confirm only what can't be undone. See [Destructive actions](?path=/docs/guides-destructive-actions--docs).
- A tour, an onboarding checklist or a multi-step form can be left, skipped or finished later, and Back, Cancel and Escape keep people's work.
- Explain before the browser's prompt for the camera, location or notifications. See [Permission request](?path=/docs/patterns-permission-request--docs).
- Make the private choice the default.

## Build on what people already know

Use familiar patterns, and solve the same problem the same way everywhere.

**In practice**

- Native elements first: `<select>`, `<dialog>`, `<input>` bring the phone's picker, autofill and screen-reader support.
- × closes, Escape cancels, Mod+Z undoes, the main action comes last.
- Components use tokens, never raw values; similar components share sizes, states, keys and words.
- A disabled control says why, and a change on screen is announced to screen readers.

## Design for everyone from the start

People use different devices, inputs and abilities. Treat accessibility as a requirement, not an afterthought.

**In practice**

- WCAG 2.2 AA in every theme, checked by tests. When principles conflict, accessibility comes first.
- Color is never the only signal: a status has a word, a chart series has a shape.
- Targets are at least 24px, and 44px on touch screens. Nothing appears only on hover.
- State people expect to keep survives a reload. See [Keeping state](?path=/docs/patterns-page-shell--docs).
- Ship only what the page needs: no runtime dependencies beyond React, and a size budget for every component.

## Care about every detail

People may never name the details, but they feel them. Let every detail add delight, never distraction.

**In practice**

- Each component covers rest, hover, focus, pressed, invalid, read-only, disabled, loading and empty, at every size and in every theme.
- Sizes line up across components, corners nest, numbers don't jump, and motion is short and stops for `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Changes follow semantic versioning, with migration notes for anything that breaks.

## Influences

These principles draw on Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, Dieter Rams' ten principles for good design, Jakob Nielsen's usability heuristics, the Gestalt principles, the Laws of UX, Microsoft's inclusive design, and the design principles of Shopify Polaris, IBM Carbon, Atlassian, Google Material and Vercel Geist.
