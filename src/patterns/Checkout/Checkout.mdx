# Checkout

The page where an order is paid for: a delivery address, a delivery speed, an optional tip and card details, with the order summary beside them. "Review your order" checks everything at once, then shows it all before anything is charged.

<Canvas of={Stories.Details} />

## When to use it

- Someone is paying for something they've chosen, and it has to reach them.
- Use it for a single order. A subscription sign-up has a plan choice first; a donation is one amount and a card.
- Not for collecting card details to keep without a payment: say so on the page, and leave out the order summary.

## What each part is for

- **The autocomplete tokens** on every field about the person (`shipping name`, `shipping address-line1`, `shipping postal-code`, `shipping country`, `email`, `tel`, `cc-number`, `cc-name`, `cc-exp`, `cc-csc`). The browser and password managers fill them, so nobody types an address or a card number they've typed before (1.3.5).
- **Labels that stay**, and hints for what's hard: where the card number is, the expiry date's form ("MM/YY, like 08/27"), where the security code is. Optional fields say "optional", so every other one is needed.
- **One check, all at once.** "Review your order" checks every field. An ErrorSummary takes focus and links to each field, and each field says what to write, not just that it's wrong (3.3.1, 3.3.3). A second try with the same problems takes focus again.
- **A Radio group for the delivery speed**, with the price in the label and the days in the description, and **a NumberInput for the tip** in the order's currency, in its minor units (`minorUnits`), so $2.50 is never read as 250 and the sums don't drift.
- **Every amount in minor units** of `currency`, as integers: prices, `expressPrice`, the tip and the total. That's cents for USD, but whole yen for JPY and fils (a thousandth) for KWD, so ￥1,800 is `1800`, not `180000`.
- **The order summary** beside the form (below it on a phone) with every line, the delivery, the tip and the total, updated as they change.
- **A review step** before anything is charged: the address, the delivery and the card (only its last four digits), each with a "Change" button that goes back with every answer kept and focus on that part's first field (3.3.4, 3.3.7). "Place order" says the amount, and comes after the summary, so on a phone every line of the total is passed before the button that pays.
- **Focus on each new step's heading**, so a screen reader reads where it is (2.4.3). Placing the order is announced; a failure keeps the review and says nothing was charged.
- **A skip link** first, into main, as on every page.

## What stays your app's job

- **Taking the payment.** Use your payment provider's own card fields where it requires them (they keep card numbers off your server), its 3-D Secure step, and its own error messages, put into words a person can act on. `onPlaceOrder` gets the order; reject its promise when the payment fails.
- **Checking the address** with a postcode lookup, and the countries you deliver to, with each country's own address fields.
- **Prices, taxes and the order number**, from your server. The summary here adds up what it's given.
- **Saved addresses and cards**: pass them as `initial`, so a returning customer goes straight to the review.
