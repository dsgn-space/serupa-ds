# Permission request

Asks the browser for the camera (or the microphone, location, notifications, the clipboard) only when someone chooses to use it, says why just before, and keeps the page working when the answer is no.

<Canvas of={Stories.BeforeAsking} />

## When to use it

- A feature needs something the browser guards with its own question: the camera or microphone, location, notifications, reading the clipboard.

## When not to use it

- Not on page load, and not in onboarding before the feature is in sight: a question with no reason in view gets a no, and many browsers then stop asking for good.
- Not for cookies: that's the [Consent banner](?path=/docs/patterns-consent-banner--docs).

## What each part is for

- **Asked at the moment of use.** The browser's question opens only after a press on "Turn on camera", next to the thing it's for. Until then the page works without it: "Join with audio only" is there from the start.
- **Why, first, in a sentence.** The card says what the camera is for and that it can be turned off later. One button opens the browser's own question; there's no second action beside it, and nothing on the card is dressed up to look like the browser's prompt.
- **Waiting, said.** While the browser asks, the button shows it's busy and says "Waiting for your browser…", so a press that seems to do nothing isn't pressed again.
- **Each answer, said**: "Camera on", "Camera blocked by the browser", "This browser can't share a camera" (4.1.3). The button that asked goes away, so focus moves on to Join, the next step, rather than back to the top of the page (2.4.3).
- **A no is not a dead end.** A warning says where to change it (the site settings by the address) with Try again, and the call still goes ahead with audio only. A browser with no camera at all gets an info message, not an error, since nothing went wrong.
- **A skip link** first, into main, as on every page.

## What stays your app's job

- **Asking the browser.** `request` stands in for it: call `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia({ video: true })` (or `Notification.requestPermission()`, `navigator.geolocation`…) and resolve with `'granted'`, `'denied'`, or `'unsupported'` when the API isn't there. Using the stream, and stopping it when the call ends, is yours too.
- **Starting in the right place.** Read the Permissions API (`navigator.permissions.query`) on load and pass its state as `initial`, so someone who said yes before isn't asked again and someone who said no sees how to change it.
- **The words.** Name your own feature and what it does with the camera, in one sentence.
