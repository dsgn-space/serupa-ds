# Return request

Sending back some of an order: which items and why, how they go back, where the money goes, and what happens next.

<Canvas of={Stories.Default} />

## When to use it

- A page reached from an order, to return or exchange what was in it.

## When not to use it

- Not for canceling an order that hasn't shipped: the Order status pattern does that in one step.
- Not for a problem that needs a person: that's the Contact form pattern.

## What each part is for

- **The items are a named group of checkboxes.** One that can't be returned is disabled and says why next to it (1.3.1, 3.3.2).
- **The reason is asked for only for an item you've chosen**, and labeled with its name: "Why are you returning the Linen shirt?" (3.3.2, 2.4.6).
- **Sending with something missing shows a summary at the top that takes focus**, each problem a link to its item or reason, and each says its own problem too (3.3.1, 2.4.3).
- **How it goes back and where the money goes are named radio groups**, and a cost is said in the choice's own words (1.3.1).
- **The refund is worked out as you choose**, with a pickup's cost taken off by name, and none for an item that arrived damaged or wrong.
- **Start return shows it's busy**; one that fails says so next to the button and keeps every choice (3.3.4, 4.1.3).
- **Once sent, the next steps take focus**: how to send it, by when, and when the money comes back (2.4.3).
- **A skip link** first, into main, as on every page.

<Canvas of={Stories.SendFails} />

## What stays your app's job

- **Making the return.** `onSend` gets the items with their reasons, how they go back and where the refund goes: resolve with the return's number, or reject.
- **What can be returned.** Set `notReturnable` on an item with the reason in words; the pattern disables it.
- **The refund.** The pattern adds it up for show; the server works out what's paid back.
- **"Error: " in the page's `<title>`** while the error summary shows, so a reload says it first (3.3.1).
