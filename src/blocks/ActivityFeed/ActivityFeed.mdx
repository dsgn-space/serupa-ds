# Activity feed

Who did what and when, newest first, with Load more at the end. It's built from Timeline, Avatar, RelativeTime and LoadMore.

<Canvas of={Stories.Default} />

## What each part is for

- **A region named by its heading** (1.3.1). Screen reader users can jump straight to "Activity". Pick `headingLevel` to fit the page.
- **The events are an ordered list**, so a screen reader says how many there are and can move between them.
- **Who did it is in words.** Each line reads as a sentence, "Ana Putri commented on Checkout redesign". The avatar beside the name is decoration, so it's hidden from screen readers and the name isn't said twice.
- **When is a real time** (RelativeTime). People read "5 minutes ago", machines read the exact moment, and the full date is in the tooltip. Older events show the date instead.
- **The marker's color is extra.** `status` colors an event's marker by its kind, but the words already say what happened.
- **Load more keeps your place** (2.4.3, 4.1.3). New events land above the button, focus stays on it, and screen readers hear "2 more loaded. Showing 6 of 6." After the last page the button gives way to "You’ve reached the end", and focus moves there, so it's never lost.
- **Another language**: `title`, `emptyText`, `loadMoreLabel` and `endText` are props, and each event's `action` is your own words. When the language puts the target inside the sentence, make `action` a function that gets the target, link included: `action: (t) => <>hat {t} kommentiert</>`. LoadMore's announcement follows the locale.

## What stays your app's job

- Fetching the events and the next page, and keeping them when people come back.
- Writing each action in words that fit your product: "commented on", "closed", "joined the team".
- Telling people when a load fails, with an Alert and a way to try again.
